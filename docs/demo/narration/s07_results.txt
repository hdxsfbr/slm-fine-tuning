The numbers. On thirty hand-written challenge conversations, judged by the same rubric as the ablation, the base passes none, the best frontier prompt passes twenty percent, the first tuned model fifty, and the second, trained on 805 conversations from a frontier teacher, seventy.

On one hundred and two held-out positions it passes ninety-three percent. A data-efficiency curve over four dataset sizes and two seeds shows that the second half of the data buys the hard negatives, and a second judge family keeps the same ordering: base, then prompt, then tuned.
